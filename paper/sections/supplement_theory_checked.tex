\section{Decision Guarantees and the Limit of OEH}
\label{supp:theory-checked}
This section restates, in the notation of the main text (decision threshold $q$, penalty $\kappa$), the statements of the checked theory appendix (\url{paper/appendix_theory.tex}); numerical checks are in \url{artifacts/week8/theory_checks_2026-10-15}.

\textbf{Setting.} A cumulative forecast is $F=(F_0,\ldots,F_5)$ with $F_j=\Pr(E\le j)$, non-decreasing in $j$. Collecting budget $b_a$ ($a\le5$) costs $b_a$ plus $\kappa$ if $E>a$; skipping costs $\kappa$ if $E\le5$. Under $F$ the expected costs are $C(a;F)=b_a+\kappa(1-F_a)$ and $C(\mathrm{skip};F)=\kappa F_5$, and the plug-in Bayes rule $a(F)$ minimizes them. Let $\mathcal G$ be an information set to which $F$ is measurable and $F^\star_j=\Pr(E\le j\mid\mathcal G)$; on the final panels $\mathcal G$ is (MDP, behavior, pilot) and $F^\star$ is the per-seed plug-in law of the ten training seeds.

\textbf{Theorem S1 (regret from scoring).} On every row, $C(a(F);F^\star)-C(a(F^\star);F^\star)\le2\kappa\|F-F^\star\|_\infty$. With $\Delta=\mathbb E[\mathrm{IBS}(F,E)]-\mathbb E[\mathrm{IBS}(F^\star,E)]\ge0$, the expected regret is at most $2\kappa\,\mathbb E\|F-F^\star\|_\infty\le2\kappa\sqrt{6\Delta}$.
\emph{Proof.} For every action, $|C(a;F)-C(a;F^\star)|\le\kappa\|F-F^\star\|_\infty$. Writing $\hat a=a(F)$, $a^\star=a(F^\star)$, the regret is $[C(\hat a;F^\star)-C(\hat a;F)]+[C(\hat a;F)-C(a^\star;F)]+[C(a^\star;F)-C(a^\star;F^\star)]$; the middle term is non-positive and the outer terms are at most $\kappa\|F-F^\star\|_\infty$. For the expectation, $\max_j|x_j|\le\|x\|_2$ and Jensen's inequality give $\mathbb E\|F-F^\star\|_\infty\le(\mathbb E\|F-F^\star\|_2^2)^{1/2}$; for each $j$, $\mathbb E[(F_j-\mathbf 1\{E\le j\})^2]=\mathbb E[(F_j-F^\star_j)^2]+\mathbb E[(F^\star_j-\mathbf 1\{E\le j\})^2]$ because the cross term vanishes by conditioning on $\mathcal G$, so $\mathbb E\|F-F^\star\|_2^2=6\Delta$. $\square$

\textbf{Proposition S2 (registered threshold rule).} Let $a_q(F)$ be the smallest $a\le5$ with $F_a\ge q$, or skip. For every $m\in(0,1]$, the expected regret of $a_q(F)$ relative to $a_q(F^\star)$ is at most $(b_5+\kappa)[\Pr(\|F-F^\star\|_\infty\ge m)+\Pr(\min_j|F^\star_j-q|<m)]\le(b_5+\kappa)[6\Delta/m^2+\Pr(\min_j|F^\star_j-q|<m)]$.
\emph{Proof.} The rule depends on $F$ only through $\mathbf 1\{F_j\ge q\}$. If $\|F-F^\star\|_\infty<m\le\min_j|F^\star_j-q|$, every $F_j$ lies on the same side of $q$ as $F^\star_j$, so the decisions coincide. Every expected cost under $F^\star$ lies in $[0,b_5+\kappa]$; Markov's inequality and the identity above give the second form. $\square$ The difference can be negative, since the threshold rule is not optimal under $F^\star$.

\textbf{Corollary S3 (log-loss form).} If all class probabilities of $F$ are positive, the excess log loss equals $\Delta_{\rm LL}=\mathbb E\,\mathrm{KL}(p^\star\|p)$, and the expected plug-in regret is at most $\kappa\sqrt{2\Delta_{\rm LL}}$. \emph{Proof.} $|F_j-F^\star_j|\le\mathrm{TV}(p,p^\star)\le\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}(p^\star\|p)/2}$ by Pinsker's inequality; combine with Theorem S1 and Jensen's inequality. $\square$

\textbf{Numerical check.} On the four Test-1 panels ($\kappa=100$, $q=0.8$), plug-in regrets are 4.3--35.9 budget units against 24--101 for the $L_\infty$ form and 51--234 for the IBS form; threshold-rule regrets are 4.4--34.2 against 55--127 for the event form minimized over $m$; Corollary S3 gives 62--155. All bounds hold and are loose.

\textbf{Proposition S4 (local constancy and gate saturation).} Let $\phi_n$ be the encoded features of a pilot of $n$ transitions from a fixed MDP and behavior, and fix the fitted heads. If every coordinate converges almost surely in $[-\infty,\infty]$ to $\phi^\star$ and no finite coordinate of $\phi^\star$ lies on a split threshold or a float32 rounding boundary, then every head output is eventually equal to its value at $\phi^\star$; if moreover $d_n\to d^\star\in[0,\infty]$, the OEH law converges to the gate at these outputs and $d^\star$. \emph{Proof.} Each tree comparison is eventually constant (a finite limit is off every threshold; an infinite limit eventually passes all of them), so every leaf, raw output and fixed convex combination is eventually constant; the gate is continuous in $d$ on $[0,\infty]$ because $\lambda$ and $\varepsilon$ are constant for $d\ge d_0$. $\square$

\textbf{Scope.} The log pilot size and the log smallest positive count grow without bound, so $d_n\to\infty$ and the law converges to $(1-\varepsilon_{\max})[(1-p_6)q_R,\,p_6]+\varepsilon_{\max}\bar p_l$. The premise holds for B2 and, under no-tie and no-threshold-hit conditions, for OEH-H; it is assumed for the posterior concentrations and the diagnostics D, and holds for OEH-B only in the idealized $B\to\infty$ form. The proposition is therefore not a consistency result: OEH defers to the robust head for pilots far larger than those of meta-training. A finite-candidate selection bound (Hoeffding plus a union bound over 500 gate candidates and three external panels) gives a slack larger than the loss range and is not used; the nested leave-one-panel-out estimate of Section~\ref{supp:posthoc} replaces it.
