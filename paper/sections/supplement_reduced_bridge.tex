\section{Earlier Reduced-Method BRIDGE MiniGrid Audit}
We evaluated a previously defined ablation omitting direct OEH-B features on 14 held-out published BRIDGE MiniGrid MDPs with five behavior policies each. The MDPs are third-party artifacts; our protocol generated the offline logs and seven-class requirement labels. Both forecast arms were sealed before the 210 labels were computed. Table~\ref{tab:bridge-reduced} is an internal-method comparison on external MDPs: B2 matched and the reduced ablation use the same forecasting architecture and target. It is neither a test of OEH-F, the full 108-field head, nor an end-to-end comparison with another author's predictor.

The reduced-minus-B2 paired Brier differences, with task-cluster bootstrap 95\% intervals, are BC $+0.02210$ [$+0.00720,+0.03780$], CFQI $-0.07380$ [$-0.09698,-0.04943$], and IQL $-0.01014$ [$-0.01805,-0.00244$]. Log loss has the same direction for each learner. Censoring beyond $x50$ is 90.0\% for BC, 60.0\% for CFQI, and 87.1\% for IQL. The prespecified $q=0.8$ budget decision does not improve target attainment: BC and CFQI tie B2 at 10.0\% and 40.0\%, while IQL decreases from 12.9\% to 11.4\%. These separate bootstrap intervals do not constitute a simultaneous across-learner superiority test. OEH-F was stopped before test labels on these 14 tasks by a pre-outcome computation-resource gate. It was run later, after the labels had been opened; Section~\ref{sec:bridge-oehf} reports that run as exploratory.

A post-outcome exploratory ranking audit compares this reduced OEH-F ablation with the original TQ score on the rows where TQ is defined (55 pilot groups from 11 tasks). Spearman $\rho$ for reduced OEH-F/TQ is $0.498/0.482$ for BC, $0.762/0.555$ for CFQI, and $0.578/0.577$ for IQL. Paired task-cluster intervals for BC and IQL differences include zero. ERI, SACo, and author-published EH descriptors were also evaluated as original-score or oracle diagnostics, with unequal information access and undefined rates; the accompanying audit reports all rows. Neither this analysis nor the OEH-F computation gate supports a claim that OEH-F is the best forecaster on BRIDGE.
