\section{Colosseum confirmation panel in detail}
\label{supp:colosseum}
The first Colosseum panel drew 72 MDPs with the authors' sampler of the Colosseum benchmark~\cite{conserva2022hardness} (seed 2027; RiverSwim, DeepSea, FrozenLake and SimpleGrid, 18 each; $16\le SA\le256$; mean rewards; $\gamma=0.95$), excluding the kernels of an earlier 32-MDP exploratory run. Its protocol registered two contrasts: B2 matched and B2 plus Colosseum's own hardness measures (diameter, value norm and gaps, which read the true MDP). \OEH{} improved on them by 24.8\%/9.3\% and 29.0\%/12.9\% (IBS/LL); with the team's max-$t$ rule the bounds against B2 are $+0.0151$ and $+0.0003$. Table~\ref{tab:colosseum} adds comparators computed after the outcomes. The pilot bundle was later regenerated from the sampler (all 360 pilot hashes equal the sealed ones), which made the authors' TQ/SACo code~\cite{schweighofer2022dataset} and the learning-curve inputs computable. With all eleven cohort-7 comparators, Tier~1 and Tier~2 are NO-GO: B2 plus ERI is not beaten (IBS $-0.0059$, LL $-0.0952$ bounds), and the privileged B2 plus TQ/SACo is slightly better than \OEH{}. \OEH{} detects requirements beyond $x50$ best (AUC 0.915 vs 0.855 for B2), while the B2 + D arm places finite requirements best (62\% within one level vs 52\%). On RiverSwim the horizon block, computed on the posterior-mean model with zero reward for unseen keys, worsens LL (Shapley $-0.151$ $[-0.262,-0.040]$). Forty-two of 360 pairs accepted no posterior draw; their labels were almost all censored, so the fallback did not cause the RiverSwim failure.
