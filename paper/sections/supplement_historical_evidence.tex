\section{Historical Gates and Narrow Component Test}
The archived forecast registry of cohort 7 had 22 contrasts. All had positive mean improvements and positive simultaneous lower bounds under its original correction. BWD was part of that registry, but its tabular adaptation is excluded from the current comparison because it does not preserve the original continuous-action geometry. The other 20 archived contrasts retain positive bounds under the same correction; excluding BWD after observing outcomes does not create a new confirmatory gate. Against the privileged TQ/SACo augmentation, the relative gains were 11.92\% Brier and 8.32\% log loss, with lower bounds $+0.00101$ and $+0.02281$. The privileged-information and combined gates failed under their earlier protocol. The raw, unfitted OEH-B row scored $0.09783$ Brier and $0.91068$ log loss on the primary panel of cohort 7 and is not a calibrated stand-alone predictor. The 36 deterministic controls had \OEH{} scores of $0.02087$ and $0.28573$; they were descriptive rather than part of the stochastic gate.

The registered Farama Minari FourRooms random-trajectory component experiment~\cite{farama_minari_fourrooms} reserved 990 episodes for fitting and 2,084 for testing, leaving 7,100 unused. The task predicts the next local object category from a visual abstraction, not a learner's seven-category data requirement. The Dirichlet empirical-Bayes model scored $0.03492/0.08801$ Brier/log loss, versus $0.02972/0.08933$ for maximum likelihood and $0.05417/0.15269$ for Krichevsky--Trofimov smoothing. Its simultaneous Brier bound versus maximum likelihood was negative, as was the log-loss lower bound. The registered four-contrast gate was NO-GO. Partial observations place this experiment outside the full tabular learner-requirement claim.
