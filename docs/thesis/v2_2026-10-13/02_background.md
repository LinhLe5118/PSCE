# 2. Theoretical Background and Problem Definition

## 2.1. Reinforcement Learning and Offline RL

A finite discounted Markov decision process (MDP) is a tuple $M=(\mathcal S,\mathcal A,P,R,\gamma,\rho)$ [@puterman1994; @sutton2018]. $\mathcal S$ is a finite set of $S=|\mathcal S|$ states and $\mathcal A$ a finite set of $A=|\mathcal A|$ actions. In state $s$, taking action $a$ moves the system to $s'$ with probability $P(s'\mid s,a)$ and yields the reward $R(s,a,s')$. The discount factor $\gamma\in(0,1)$ weights future rewards and $\rho$ is the initial-state distribution. Some states are terminal: once reached, the episode ends and no further reward accrues. The MDP is *stochastic* when some transition rows put mass on more than one next state.

A stationary policy $\pi(a\mid s)$ chooses actions. Its value function and discounted return are

$$V^\pi(s)=\mathbb E_\pi\Big[\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^t R(s_t,a_t,s_{t+1})\,\Big|\,s_0=s\Big],\qquad J(\pi)=\sum_s\rho(s)V^\pi(s).$$ (2.1)

The action value $Q^\pi(s,a)$ is the expected return after taking $a$ in $s$ and following $\pi$. The optimal values satisfy the Bellman optimality equation

$$Q^*(s,a)=\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\big[R(s,a,s')+\gamma\max_{a'}Q^*(s',a')\big],$$ (2.2)

and a greedy policy with respect to $Q^*$ is optimal, with return $J^*$. Repeated application of the right-hand side, the Bellman update, is a $\gamma$-contraction, so value iteration converges from any start [@sutton2018]. In a tabular MDP, $V^\pi$ can be computed exactly by solving a linear system; the study uses this exact evaluation to score policies.

In *offline* RL the learner does not interact with $M$. It receives a dataset $D=\{(s_i,a_i,r_i,s'_i)\}$ of transitions generated by a behavior policy $\beta$ and must output a policy $\hat\pi$ [@levine2020]. The *target* policy is the one the learner outputs; the behavior policy is the one that generated the data. The central difficulty is missing support: if an action is never logged in a state, the data contain no information about its reward or successor. A learner that trusts extrapolated values for such actions can choose them and fail. Offline learners therefore restrict or penalize unsupported actions, and theory expresses the data requirement through how well the data cover the state–action distribution of a good policy [@rashidinejad2021; @li2024].

## 2.2. Terminology and Notation of This Study

The following terms have a fixed meaning throughout the thesis.

- **Learner** $l$: a registered offline training procedure that maps a buffer of transitions to a policy (here BC, CFQI or IQL, Section 4.6). A learner is not a policy.
- **Pilot** $D_0$: an independent log of $N_0=10SA$ transitions, written $x10$, collected by $\beta$ before the forecast.
- **Transition and trajectory**: one tuple $(s,a,r,s')$; an episode of at most 100 consecutive transitions, restarted from $\rho$.
- **Budget** $N_j$: a total number of transitions $b_jSA$ with $b=(1,2,5,10,20,50)$, $j=0,\ldots,5$. Budgets are totals, not data added to the pilot.
- **Nested prefix**: within one training stream, the budget-$x1$ buffer is the first $SA$ transitions of the budget-$x2$ buffer, and so on. Larger budgets extend smaller ones.
- **Normalized return** $\mathrm{NR}$: the return rescaled so that a uniformly random policy scores 0 and the optimal policy scores 1 (Eq. 2.4).
- **Sustained success**: success at a budget and at every larger measured budget, judged on ten seeds.
- **Empirical requirement** $E_l$: the first budget index with sustained success, or the censored class 6 (Eq. 2.6).
- **Censoring**: the outcome $>x50$ records that the requirement lies beyond the measured grid; its exact value is unknown.
- **Posterior**: a probability distribution over MDPs and behavior policies that are compatible with the pilot under stated priors.
- **Oracle**: any quantity computed with the true MDP, such as $J^*$ or the true effective horizon. Oracles are used to score and to define privileged comparators, never as forecast inputs.
- **Forecaster**: a model that maps a pilot and a learner identity to seven class probabilities.

## 2.3. Inputs and Outputs of the Problem

The study has two levels that must not be confused. At the **learner level**, an offline learner receives a buffer and returns a policy; we observe whether that policy succeeds. At the **forecaster level**, the forecaster receives the pilot $D_0$, the learner identity $l$ and public metadata ($S$, $A$, $\rho$, $\gamma$, the budget grid), and returns a probability vector

$$\hat p_l=(\hat p_{l,0},\ldots,\hat p_{l,6}),\qquad \hat p_{l,c}\ge0,\quad \sum_{c=0}^{6}\hat p_{l,c}=1,$$ (2.3)

where $\hat p_{l,c}$ forecasts $\Pr(E_l=c)$. The forecaster itself is fitted beforehand on *meta-training* data: earlier cohorts of MDPs for which pilots and requirement labels are already known. At test time it is applied to pilots from new MDPs whose learner outcomes have not been observed. The forecast for a test row is then compared with the label measured on independent training streams of the same MDP and behavior.

## 2.4. Data Requirement and Success Criterion

For a policy $\pi$ the normalized return is

$$\mathrm{NR}(\pi)=\frac{J(\pi)-J_{\mathrm{random}}}{J^*-J_{\mathrm{random}}},$$ (2.4)

where $J_{\mathrm{random}}$ is the return of the uniformly random policy. The denominator is positive by construction of the benchmark. $\mathrm{NR}$ is not a probability and is not clipped: a policy worse than random has a negative value. A training seed *succeeds* when $\mathrm{NR}\ge\tau$ with $\tau=0.95$.

For learner $l$, budget index $j$ and seed $i=1,\ldots,10$, let $\pi_{l,j,i}$ be the policy trained on the budget-$j$ prefix of training stream $i$. The success count and the empirical requirement are

$$C_{l,j}=\sum_{i=1}^{10}\mathbf 1\{\mathrm{NR}(\pi_{l,j,i})\ge\tau\},$$ (2.5)

$$E_l=\min\Big\{j\in\{0,\ldots,5\}:\ \min_{m\ge j}C_{l,m}\ge 8\Big\},\qquad E_l=6\ \text{if the set is empty}.$$ (2.6)

The suffix condition is essential because learning curves are not monotone. Consider the success counts $(2,5,8,7,9,10)$ at $x1,\ldots,x50$. The first budget with eight successes is $x5$, but the count falls to seven at $x10$, so $x5$ is not sustained; the requirement is $x20$ ($E_l=4$). Counts $(10,10,10,10,10,7)$ give $E_l=6$: early success that is lost at the largest budget is not sustained. Counts $(0,0,0,0,0,8)$ give $x50$. In the test cohort, 21.0% of primary rows show at least one decrease of the success count between consecutive budgets (Section 3.6), so a first-crossing rule would mislabel many rows.

The class $E_l=6$ is *right censoring*: the requirement exceeds $x50$ or does not exist within any budget. It is not an infinite requirement, and it is not a technical failure. Because the grid is finite, $E_l$ identifies an interval of budgets rather than an exact number of transitions, and because ten seeds are used, it is a noisy estimate of the population success rate.

The pilot itself costs $x10$. Forecasts of the classes $x1$, $x2$ and $x5$ therefore describe how an *independent* training stream behaves at those sizes; they are retrospective and do not demonstrate data saved before the pilot was collected.

## 2.5. Assumptions and Information Access

The study assumes Markov dynamics, finite and known state and action sets, a stationary behavior policy during logging, and fixed learner implementations. The pilot and the ten training streams of each MDP–behavior pair are generated from disjoint seed namespaces, so the pilot never overlaps the training data.

Information access is restricted by role.

- **Forecaster.** Reads the pilot, the public metadata and the meta-training model. It never receives the true transition kernel, the true behavior policy, $J^*$, $J_{\mathrm{random}}$ or any test outcome.
- **Learners.** BC, CFQI, IQL, BCQ and CQL fit only the logged buffer and its dimensions. SPIBB receives the true behavior policy $\beta$, and VI-LCB receives terminal-state metadata (Section 4.6). These are disclosed because they change what a comparison means.
- **Selector (secondary).** Additionally receives the generator-family label of each MDP, which a pilot does not reveal.
- **Scoring.** Uses the true MDP to evaluate every policy exactly and to define the labels. Knowing the true MDP at scoring time does not give it to any forecaster.

Three notions of support must be kept apart: actions *observed* in the pilot, the *true* support of $\beta$, and the support of a *posterior* behavior model, which can include actions never observed because the prior smooths the counts. No pilot-only statistic can identify the value of an unlogged action: two MDPs that agree on every logged action but differ on an unlogged one generate the same distribution of pilots. Any forecaster must therefore adopt a convention for unobserved rewards; OEH assigns them zero (Section 4.3). This is a modeling choice, not a property that is guaranteed to hold.
